Required training and age-appropriate elementary instruction

Last updated: September 27, 2026

Goal

Require regular training for teachers, administrators, and school board members on Iowa’s recent education laws, including diversity, equity, and inclusion prohibitions, parental rights, transparency, and related reforms. Keep elementary instruction age-appropriate, including on pronouns and related topics.

Current law

Iowa already limits some training and curriculum content, and it already requires other kinds of staff and board training. It does not require training on the education statutes listed in the gap section below.

Iowa Code and Administrative Code provisions this priority would amend or use as a model. Links open the current official PDF.
Section What it does now
Iowa Code 279.74 The superintendent must ensure that curriculum and mandatory staff or student training do not teach, advocate, encourage, promote, or act upon race or sex stereotyping or scapegoating. Those “specific defined concepts” are listed in section 261H.8. The section contains broad savings clauses. Subsection 279.74(4)(d) says the section creates no enforceable right. There is no penalty and no referral path to the Board of Educational Examiners.
279.75 and 256.9(64) Annual First Amendment training for any equity coordinator, using a program developed by the Department of Education. 2026 House File 2670 kept the training and struck the department’s standards of practice for equity coordinators.
19.1, 19.2, and 19.3 No public money for diversity, equity, and inclusion offices or officers by any “state entity,” which includes political subdivisions and therefore school districts. The attorney general may seek a writ of mandamus, and a student, alumnus, or employee may seek an injunction. Section 19.1(1)(e) lists concepts, including “transgender ideology,” “neopronouns,” and “gender theory.”
279.78 A school may not give a parent false or misleading information about a student’s gender identity. If a student asks a licensed employee to use a different name or pronoun as a gender-identity accommodation, the employee must tell an administrator, and the administrator must tell the parent. The Department of Education investigates: a written warning for a first violation, and a Board of Educational Examiners hearing for a second knowing violation.
279.80 No program, curriculum, test, survey, questionnaire, promotion, or instruction relating to gender theory or sexual orientation in kindergarten through grade 6. The statute has no enforcement mechanism.
256.11(19) Defines “age-appropriate” for the educational program. The definition excludes material with descriptions or visual depictions of a sex act as defined in section 702.17.
4.1A Rules of construction from 2025 Senate File 418. “Sex,” “male,” and “female” are defined, and “gender” is a synonym for sex, not gender identity.
21.12 The only existing statutory training mandate for school board members. A newly elected or appointed member of a governmental body must complete one to two hours of open-meetings and open-records training within 90 days. The Iowa Public Information Board provides or approves the training. Completion certificates are public records. Damages apply for noncompliance.
279.70, 279.72, and 232.69 Existing staff-training mandates on suicide prevention, dyslexia, and mandatory child-abuse reporting. They are structural models for a new training mandate.
279.14A and chapter 284 Professional-development notification and the teacher professional-development framework.
281 IAC 12.3(14) and 12.3(15) Department of Education accreditation rules that restate sections 279.78 and 279.80.
281 IAC 12.7(2) The professional-development standard still requires professional development to prepare staff to implement “age-appropriate, multicultural, gender-fair approaches.” 2026 House File 2670, section 6, struck the parallel “multicultural, gender-fair” language from Iowa Code section 256.11, so this rule is now out of step with the statute.
2026 House File 2754, division IX, section 76 (2026 Acts chapter 1086) A Department of Education task force is studying all state-mandated teacher trainings and license-renewal requirements. It must recommend a more manageable training-program schedule by December 31, 2026. The provision is uncodified.
2026 Senate File 2231 (2026 Acts chapter 1120), new 256.9(80) The Department of Education must distribute federal prayer and religious-expression guidance to every board member, principal, and teacher, and must develop a professional-development program. This is a recent model of statewide training on a legal update.

The gap

  • No statute requires teachers, administrators, or board members to be trained on sections 279.74, 279.78, 279.80, 256.11(9) or (19), 279.77, or chapter 19. Board members are trained only on chapters 21 and 22, under section 21.12.
  • Enforcement is uneven. Section 279.78 and the library provision in section 256.11(9)(a)(3) use a Department of Education warning, then a Board of Educational Examiners hearing. Sections 279.74 and 279.80 have no comparable path, and section 279.74(4)(d) states that the section creates no enforceable right. 2026 Senate File 2405 was offered in part to address that absence. See the bills below.
  • On pronouns, current law covers two points. Section 279.78(3) requires a report to parents when a student asks a licensed employee to use a different name or pronoun as a gender-identity accommodation. Section 279.80 bars, in kindergarten through grade 6, promotion or instruction relating to gender theory. No statute addresses staff pronoun practices or pronoun displays. Whether pronoun lessons premised on gender identity already fall under the “gender theory” language in section 279.80 has not been settled by an authoritative interpretation. That is a candidate for statutory clarification, using the definitions in section 4.1A.
  • The House File 2754 task force is charged with making training schedules more manageable. A new mandate will be weighed against that report, which is due December 31, 2026. A proposal that folds into or replaces existing trainings fits that charge more closely than one that only adds hours.

Proposed amendment approach

  1. Section 256.9 (Department of Education duties). Add a numbered subsection directing the department to develop, and update each year, one training on recent education laws. Sections 256.9(64), 256.9(69), and 256.9(80) already direct the department to develop training or model policies.
  2. Chapter 279, new section next to 279.70 and 279.72. Require annual completion by licensed staff, administrators, and board members. Borrow features from section 21.12: a deadline after taking office, free state-provided training, completion certificates, and public completion records. Section 232.69 is another completion model.
  3. Sections 279.74 and 279.80. Add an enforcement subsection mirroring section 279.78(4): Department of Education investigation, then a warning, then a Board of Educational Examiners hearing under section 256.146(13). Revisit section 279.74(4)(d), which disclaims an enforceable right.
  4. Section 279.78 or 279.80. Clarify pronoun-related instruction and practice, using the definitions in section 4.1A.
  5. Administrative rules. Ask the State Board of Education to update 281 IAC 12.7(2) so it conforms to 2026 House File 2670, and to add the training requirement to 281 IAC 12.3.

Related recent bills

Bills from the 91st General Assembly that were not enacted are no longer pending. They would have to be reintroduced. They are listed as drafting models.

2026 Senate File 2405 and House File 2116 Not enacted

Senate File 2405 was in the Senate Education Committee. House File 2116 was the House companion. The bills would have required every district employee, contractor, and volunteer who provides curriculum or training to be trained on the requirements of section 279.74, with tracking. They also would have barred spending on content that violates the section and allowed a resident, parent, or employee to seek an injunction without proof of harm. Senate File 2405 was reported out of committee and placed on the Senate calendar on February 19, 2026. It did not pass. Neither bill was enacted.

2026 House File 2123 and Senate File 2072 Not enacted

Would have amended section 19.3, including mandatory termination of violators and civil penalties, and section 279.74. It did not advance from subcommittee.

2026 Senate File 2003 and House File 2338 Not enacted
