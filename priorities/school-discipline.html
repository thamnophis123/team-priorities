Reporting potential crimes to police, and school discipline

Last updated: September 27, 2026

Goal

Require schools to report potential crimes to law enforcement, and pursue broader discipline reform.

Current law

Schools must already report some conduct to law enforcement or to the Department of Health and Human Services. For other violent or criminal incidents, reporting to police is protected if it is done in good faith. It is not required.

Reporting and discipline provisions. The 2026 removal statute is cited by Act because it is not yet in the Iowa Code 2026.
Section What it does now
Iowa Code 280.17A Required law-enforcement reporting, for weapons only. Boards must have procedures to report to local law enforcement any dangerous weapon possessed on school premises in violation of policy or law.
280.24 Required law-enforcement reporting, for drugs and alcohol. Boards must have procedures to report use or possession of alcohol or a controlled substance on school premises. Procedures may exempt a student self-report when a student-assistance program would be jeopardized.
279.51A A teacher must report a threat or an incident of violence that causes injury, property damage, or an assault to the principal within 24 hours, and parents are notified. Districts report annual incident counts to the Department of Education, which reports to the Legislature by November 1. Staff who report an incident of violence or assault to a local law enforcement agency in good faith receive whistleblower protection under section 70A.29. Reporting to police is not required.
279.65A Boards must adopt escalating discipline policies for threats and violence, including suspension, expulsion, and alternative placement, consistent with the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act and Section 504.
2026 Senate File 2428, new section 279.65B (2026 Acts chapter 1137) A teacher may remove a student for a nonviolent disruption and must remove a student for a violent disruption, defined to include injury, property damage, or an assault under section 708.1. The section covers readmission meetings and behavior plans. It does not require a report to law enforcement. It also amends section 279.65A(3). Cite the Act as well as the section number until the Iowa Code 2027 is published. Research on 2026 Acts also identifies Senate File 2428 among Acts that created a section numbered 279.89, a number used by other 2026 Acts and subject to renumbering.
280.27 Immunity for a good-faith report of violence or a threat to an appropriate person or agency.
280.25 Allows, but does not require, interagency information-sharing agreements with juvenile justice agencies, including police.
280.36 Allows multidisciplinary school safety assessment teams, which may include local law enforcement. Enacted in 2025 Senate File 583.
280.17 and chapter 232E Abuse of a student by a school employee is investigated by the Department of Health and Human Services. Employment is terminated on a founded report. The provision was rewritten by 2025 Senate File 659.
232.69 School employees are mandatory reporters of child abuse. A report is due within 24 hours. Training is required.
280.34 and 256.146(25) An incident that may be a felony by an employee goes through the Board of Educational Examiners reporting and investigation process.
279.9B A school notifies the juvenile court of an absence or suspension of a student who is under court supervision.
281 IAC 12.3(6) The discipline-policy accreditation standard. Boards must consider the potential disparate impact of the policies by race, sexual orientation, and other categories. “Disparate impact” also appears in the chapter 19 definition at section 19.1(1)(e). Whether the accreditation standard and that definition conflict is a policy question. It is not settled law.
281 IAC chapter 103 Rules on corporal punishment, restraint, and seclusion.

The gap

  • Iowa requires schools to report weapons and alcohol or controlled substances to police, and to report child abuse to the Department of Health and Human Services. No statute requires schools to report other potential crimes by students, such as an assault causing injury, sexual abuse or harassment between students, a credible threat, or significant theft or vandalism. For those incidents, current law protects voluntary reporting. See sections 279.51A(5) and 280.27. It does not require the report.
  • The annual count under section 279.51A does not track whether police were notified.
  • Federal privacy law limits sharing of education records with police. There are exceptions for health and safety emergencies and for records of a school law-enforcement unit. Under 34 C.F.R. section 300.535, a school may report a crime by a student with a disability. A draft should decide the threshold (a felony, or any offense), any age floor, and the relationship to therapeutic and alternative placements.

Proposed amendment approach

  1. Section 280.17A. Expand the existing direction that boards “prescribe procedures requiring school officials to report to local law enforcement” from weapons to an enumerated list of offenses. A new section next to 280.17A is the alternative. Boards already comply with this structure, so it is the cleanest vehicle.
  2. Section 279.51A(4) and (5). Add law-enforcement notification for defined incidents, and include police referrals in the annual report to the Department of Education.
  3. Section 279.65A and Senate File 2428’s section 279.65B. Cross-reference the reporting duty in the discipline policies and in the removal procedures.
  4. Section 280.24. Revisit the student self-report exception if it conflicts with the reporting goal.
  5. 281 IAC 12.3(6). Petition the State Board of Education to revise the discipline-policy standard.

2026 Senate File 2428 Enacted

The current discipline-reform baseline. Teachers may remove a student for a nonviolent disruption and must remove a student for a violent disruption. 2026 Acts chapter 1137. The Act does not require a report to law enforcement.
