Priority 6 of 8

Public funds for political or ideological training and displays

Last updated: September 27, 2026

Goal

Prevent school funds from being used for political or ideological training or displays, including flags.

The spending limit often cited in this discussion is Iowa Code section 68A.505, “Use of public moneys for political purposes,” in the campaign-finance chapter. It is a Code section, not an Administrative Code rule. The rules that implement it are in 351 IAC chapter 5 (Iowa Ethics and Campaign Disclosure Board, “Use of Public Resources for a Political Purpose”), re-adopted effective June 3, 2026.

Current law

Campaign-finance, diversity-office, training-content, and flag provisions. Section 68A.505 reaches elections only.
Section What it does now
Iowa Code 68A.505 The state and its political subdivisions may not spend public moneys for a political purpose, including express advocacy for or against a candidate or a ballot issue. Factual information about a ballot issue is allowed.
68A.102(19) and (14) “Political purpose” means express advocacy of the nomination, election, or defeat of a candidate, or of the passage or defeat of a ballot issue. “Express advocacy” is defined in subsection 14.
351 IAC 5.1 through 5.5 Rule 5.1 reads “public moneys” broadly to cover public resources, including staff time, equipment, and facilities. Rules 5.2 and 5.3 expressly name a public school, including a charter school. Rule 5.4 lists prohibited acts, such as using school communications to advocate a ballot measure. Rule 5.5(8) lets an employee wear campaign paraphernalia unless agency policy prohibits it.
351 IAC 5.7 and 68B.32B A complaint goes to the Ethics and Campaign Disclosure Board, which may investigate and impose sanctions.
19.1, 19.2, and 19.3 A ban on diversity, equity, and inclusion offices for a “state entity,” which includes a political subdivision, so a school district is covered. No such office or officer, and no spending on either. Carve-outs cover academic instruction, guest speakers, and legally required work. Enforcement is by attorney-general mandamus or injunction, and by a lawsuit from a student, alumnus, or employee. The statute reaches offices, officers, and hiring and admissions practices. It does not reach trainings or displays generally.
279.74 Bans teaching specified concepts of stereotyping and scapegoating in curriculum and in mandatory employee and student training. It is a content limit. It is not a spending ban, and the section itself has no enforcement mechanism. See the training priority.
261J.2 and 261J.3 Limits on diversity, equity, and inclusion offices and spending at regents universities and community colleges.
280.5 and 1B.3 Section 280.5 requires schools to fly the United States and Iowa flags on school days, to recite the Pledge of Allegiance daily in grades 1 through 12, and to display the United States flag in classrooms during the Pledge. Section 1B.3 requires the United States and Iowa flags on public buildings. Neither section limits other flags or banners. See the symbols priority.

What section 68A.505 covers

  • School districts are covered for the spending the section actually prohibits. A school district is a political subdivision, and 351 IAC chapter 5 names public schools and charter schools.
  • Non-electoral political or ideological uses are not covered. Section 68A.505 reaches express advocacy about a candidate or a ballot issue. A Pride flag, a Black Lives Matter flag, an ideological poster, or a diversity, equity, and inclusion training that does not advocate for a candidate or a ballot measure is outside the section. The Ethics Board applies the statute to election advocacy. Extending it to ideology generally would mean redefining “political purpose” across campaign-finance law.

The gap

No Iowa statute currently prohibits a school from spending funds on non-electoral political or ideological training, programs, or displays. Section 19.2 covers diversity, equity, and inclusion offices and officers only. Section 279.74 covers training content, not spending, and has no enforcement mechanism in the section itself. Flags other than the United States and Iowa flags are unaddressed.

Proposed amendment approach

  1. Section 19.2. Extend the existing spending ban from offices and officers to trainings, programming, contracts, and displays. 2026 House File 2123 is the model. It was not enacted. Section 19.2 already applies to school districts, and section 19.3 already supplies enforcement.
  2. Section 279.74. Add a spending ban on training that promotes the listed concepts, plus an enforcement mechanism. 2026 Senate File 2405 and House File 2116 are the model. They were not enacted.
  3. A new chapter 279 section on mandatory ideological training. It would bar a district from requiring or sponsoring training that makes an employee or student affirm or advocate a particular ideological viewpoint as a condition of employment, participation, evaluation, or advancement. It targets compelled affirmation rather than training topics, so it would sit alongside the content list in section 279.74 instead of replacing it. Pairing it with the section 19.2 spending extension above would cover both the requirement and the funding.
  4. Section 280.5, or a new section beside it. The new section could reference section 1B.3. This is the place to regulate flags and banners that are not governmental flags, on school property or in classrooms. Drafting constraints are on the symbols priority. A content-neutral authorized-flags list is the approach of the recent bills.
  5. Section 68A.505 is not the suggested primary vehicle. Redefining section 68A.102(19) would affect all candidate and ballot-issue law. If the Legislature uses section 68A.505, a narrow separate subsection for schools is the safer shape. The Legislative Services Agency should review either choice.
  6. Definitions. Reuse definitions already in statute, including section 19.1 and section 261H.8, so the new text is less open to a vagueness challenge.

Model legislation

A discussion draft of model language, the Public School Neutrality, Displays, and Training Act, addresses mandatory employee and student training that advocates political, racial, gender, sexual-orientation, or other ideological viewpoints. It is not a filed bill. The draft adds new sections to chapter 279 and builds on section 279.74 rather than replacing it. Operative text for the training provision will be added to this section.

Model legislation excerpt

Excerpt from a discussion draft, the Public School Neutrality, Displays, and Training Act. Not a filed bill.

Sec. 279.75(6). Definition. “Mandatory ideological training” means training required or sponsored by a school district that requires an employee or student to affirm, adopt, endorse, or participate in the advocacy of a particular ideological viewpoint as a condition of employment, educational participation, evaluation, or advancement.

Sec. 279.75(3). Definition (in part). “Ideological viewpoint” … does not include the objective teaching of established facts, historical events, scientific principles, constitutional provisions, or applicable law.

Sec. 279.76(1). Statewide policy (in part). Public schools … should provide instruction without using the authority, resources, or facilities of the state to endorse or promote a political party or ideological viewpoint.

Related recent bills

The spending bills below were not enacted. The enacted baseline is chapter 19, chapter 261J, and section 279.74, described above. Flag bills are on the symbols priority.

2026 House File 2123 and Senate File 2072 Not enacted

Would have amended section 19.3, including mandatory termination of violators and civil penalties, and section 279.74. It did not advance from subcommittee.

House File 2123 text · History

2026 Senate File 2405 and House File 2116 Not enacted

Would have required training on section 279.74, barred spending on content that violates that section, and allowed an injunction by a resident, parent, or employee without proof of harm. Senate File 2405 was placed on the Senate calendar on February 19, 2026. Neither bill was enacted.

Chapter 19 and chapter 261J Enacted
