Screen time and personal electronic devices

Last updated: September 27, 2026

Goal

Reduce excessive non-academic screen use in schools and strengthen protections for students.

Current law

Two recent enactments are the base. The 2025 law covers personal devices during instructional time. The 2026 law caps digital instruction in kindergarten through grade 5 and orders a working-group report on older grades by December 31, 2026, in time for the 2027 session.

Device and instructional-technology provisions. The 2026 section is cited by Act because it is not yet in the Iowa Code 2026, and its section number will change.
Section What it does now
Iowa Code 279.88 (2025 House File 782, 2025 Acts chapter 36) A board must adopt a policy restricting student use of a personal electronic device during instructional time. The policy must cover how a parent contacts a student, storage, discipline, and exceptions for a health need, an individualized education program or Section 504 plan, and an emergency. A board may be stricter.
256.9(69) The Department of Education had to publish a model device policy by May 1, 2025.
2026 House File 2676, division VIII (2026 Acts chapter 1129) Student instructional technology standards. Approved May 20, 2026, and effective July 1, 2026. Not yet codified. The Act numbers the new section 279.89. Senate File 2231 uses the same temporary number for a different section, and Senate File 2428 has also been identified with that number. The Code editor will renumber them. Cite 2026 Acts chapter 1129 until then.
  • In kindergarten through grade 5, instruction on a digital device is capped at 60 minutes per school day. Exceptions cover an individualized education program or Section 504 plan, assistive technology, a teacher demonstration on a projector or smartboard, assessments and screeners, and computer science.
  • Each board must adopt a kindergarten-through-grade-5 policy covering the limits, a parent’s right to request a further reduction for that parent’s child, technology that must support and not supplant instruction, and no devices at recess. The policy is posted on elementary school websites.
  • In any grade, before adopting one-to-one devices, a district must complete a checklist on educational purpose, age-appropriateness, content-filtering limits, and data practices. The checklist is kept for a Department of Education audit.
  • The standards apply to charter schools and innovation-zone schools. They exempt an online program under section 256.43.
House File 2676, section 19 A Department of Education and Department of Health and Human Services working group on screen use in grades 6 through 12. The report is due December 31, 2026.
279.71 Online privacy protections for students when a school uses a technology vendor.

The Department of Education has issued frequently asked questions and a sample policy for the new technology standards. They are described in the department’s 2026 enacted-legislation summary.

The gap

  • There is no time limit for grades 6 through 12. That question is with the working group until December 31, 2026.
  • Non-academic or recreational screen use, such as videos and games as a reward or during free time, is not addressed separately from instruction.
  • Section 279.88 restricts a personal device during instructional time only. It does not cover the full school day, including lunch and passing periods.
  • Content filtering appears as a checklist item before one-to-one adoption. There is no minimum filtering standard.

Proposed amendment approach

  1. The instructional-technology section enacted in House File 2676, once the Code editor assigns its final number. Extend the limits to grades 6 through 12 using the working-group report. Add a specific limit on non-academic screen use. Add minimum standards for content filtering.
  2. Section 279.88. Extend the personal-device restriction from instructional time to the full school day.
  3. Section 256.9(69). Update the Department of Education’s model-policy duty so the model matches the amended statutes.
