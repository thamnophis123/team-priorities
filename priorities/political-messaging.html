Goal

Address political and ideological symbols, including flags, posters, and similar displays, in public schools.

Current law

Iowa requires the United States and Iowa flags. It does not limit other flags or ideological displays. Student expression is protected by statute, including a 2026 Act that is not yet codified.

Flag, display, and student-speech provisions. Links open the current official PDF, or the 2026 Acts PDF where the section is not yet in the Code.
Section What it does now
Iowa Code 280.5 Every school site must have a flagstaff and fly the United States and Iowa flags on school days. The Pledge of Allegiance is recited daily in grades 1 through 12, with a United States flag in each classroom where it is recited. The section requires those flags. It does not limit other flags or displays. The duty is restated in 281 IAC 12.3(13).
1B.3, as amended by 2026 Senate File 2430 The custodian of a “public building” of the state or a political subdivision must raise the United States and Iowa flags, and fly them at half-staff on the governor’s proclamation. The attorney general may sue for compliance. The section does not limit other flags. 2026 Senate File 2430 (2026 Acts chapter 1095) amended this section. Those amendments are not in the Iowa Code 2026, so the Act has to be read with the Code PDF.
279.8 General school-board authority to make rules for the board’s own government and for directors, officers, employees, teachers, and pupils. Boards may already adopt display policies.
279.74 Limits curriculum and training content (specific defined concepts). It does not regulate displays.
19.1(1)(e) and 19.2 The definition of diversity, equity, and inclusion covers promoting certain concepts “as the official position of the state entity.” Section 19.2 bans funding of those offices and officers. It does not ban displays.
68A.505 and 351 IAC chapter 5 Bars the use of public resources for express advocacy in elections. See priority 6. Rule 351—5.5(8) lets a public employee wear campaign paraphernalia unless the agency adopts a policy against it.
280.22 Student free expression. Students may not distribute material that is obscene, libelous, or that incites disruption.
2026 Senate File 2231, section 8 (2026 Acts chapter 1120) Protects students’ religious, political, or ideological speech, including clothing, accessories, and jewelry that display religious, political, or ideological messages or symbols, on equal terms with other student expression. A private right of action carries a civil penalty of at least $5,000, and immunity is waived. The Act assigns this material the temporary section number 279.89. House File 2676 uses the same temporary number for a different section. Cite the Act until the Code editor renumbers it.
279.73 and 256.146(13)(e) Intellectual freedom and protection from speech-based discrimination. The Board of Educational Examiners may discipline a licensee who discriminates in violation of section 279.73.

The gap

  • No Iowa statute limits which flags, banners, or ideological symbols a school or its employees may display. The flag statutes require the United States and Iowa flags. They do not cap the list.
  • Any restriction has to keep three categories separate. Official school displays are government speech, which the Legislature can generally regulate. Employee speech depends on context: speech in an official capacity is generally regulable, and private speech has First Amendment protection. Student speech is protected by Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District, by section 280.22, and by Senate File 2231’s private-enforcement statute. A rule that reaches student clothing or student clubs would conflict with Senate File 2231.

The Legislative Services Agency and legal counsel should review any draft. Display and speech proposals raise First Amendment questions that this page only identifies.

Proposed amendment approach

  1. Section 280.5. Convert the school flag duty into a school-specific authorized-flags rule: the United States flag, the Iowa flag, and a short additional list such as the POW/MIA flag, with exceptions for curriculum and historical displays. 2026 House File 2026 took this approach. It was not enacted.
  2. Section 1B.3. A broader option, covering all public buildings, if the Legislature wants a statewide rule. 2025 House File 694 took this approach. It was not enacted. 2026 Senate File 2430 already amended section 1B.3 on half-staff display and attorney-general enforcement. A new draft has to work with that Act.
  3. Section 279.8, or a new section in chapter 279. Require each board to adopt a policy on displays by the district and by employees acting in their official capacity, in classrooms and common areas. The policy should expressly preserve section 280.22, the student-speech protections in Senate File 2231, and curricular use.
  4. New sections after section 279.74. A single statewide standard in chapter 279 that defines ideological displays, bars them as official district expression, lists approved flags, and protects classroom instruction and student speech. This combines the authorized-flags approach with a district display rule. See the model legislation below.

Model legislation

The model language below is a discussion draft, the Public School Neutrality, Displays, and Training Act. It is not a filed bill or a legal opinion. It draws on Utah’s approved-flag approach, Idaho’s limits on ideological flags and banners, and Montana’s broader limits on government displays. It adds new sections to chapter 279, after section 279.74, and keeps the flag duty in section 280.5.

The draft would:

  • Define “ideological viewpoint,” “ideological display,” and “official display.” The definition of ideological viewpoint excludes the objective teaching of established facts, historical events, scientific principles, constitutional provisions, and applicable law.
  • Bar a school district from displaying, authorizing, purchasing, or maintaining an ideological flag, banner, sign, poster, emblem, or other display when it is presented as the district’s official expression. The rule covers school buildings and grounds, district websites, official social media, and newsletters, and displays put up by employees, contractors, or agents acting in an official capacity.
  • List approved flags: the United States and Iowa flags; flags of Iowa counties, cities, and other political subdivisions; flags of federally recognized tribes; United States Armed Forces flags, including POW/MIA; school and district flags; flags of other countries shown for an educational or diplomatic purpose; Olympic and Paralympic flags; historical flags used temporarily in an approved course; and any flag the General Assembly or Department of Education rules authorize.
  • Apply the same rule regardless of viewpoint. A district could not allow a display on one side of an issue while banning a comparable display on the other side.
  • Let a teacher use a flag or display temporarily as a teaching aid tied to an approved course, removed when the lesson ends.
  • Reach official displays only. The draft states that listing an example does not make an individual’s identity, beliefs, or protected expression prohibited.

Drafting notes: Check the new section numbers against the 2026 Acts before introduction, because several 2026 Acts added chapter 279 sections. The Legislative Services Agency and legal counsel should review the text for First Amendment issues, especially the list of examples in subsection 4.

Model legislation excerpt

Excerpt from a discussion draft, the Public School Neutrality, Displays, and Training Act. Not a filed bill.

Sec. 279.76(2). Prohibited official displays. A school district shall not display, authorize, purchase, or maintain an ideological flag, banner, sign, poster, emblem, or other ideological display on public school property when the display is presented as an official expression of the school district.

Sec. 279.76(3). Approved flags. Notwithstanding subsection 2, a school district may display: a. The official flag of the United States. b. The official flag of the State of Iowa. … e. The official flag of a branch or unit of the United States Armed Forces, including POW/MIA flags. f. An official flag of a public school, school district, or accredited educational institution. … i. A historical flag displayed temporarily as part of an approved course of academic instruction.

Sec. 279.76(5). Uniform application. A school district shall apply this section consistently without favoring or disfavoring a viewpoint because of its political, religious, social, or ideological content.

Sec. 279.76(6). Temporary educational displays. This subsection does not prohibit the display of historical flags, political symbols, or ideological materials for the purpose of teaching history, civics, government, literature, religion, or other academic subjects.

Models from other states

Utah, H.B. 77 (2025), Flag Display Amendments Enacted

Utah Code 63G-1-704. Became law March 27, 2025, effective May 7, 2025. Government entities, including public schools and school employees on duty, may display only 13 listed categories of flags on government property. The state auditor investigates and allows 30 days to cure. Non-school entities that do not cure face $500 per violation per day, and schools are not fined. Student expression is preserved.

Bill · Enrolled text · Code section

Idaho, H.B. 41 (2025) Enacted

2025 Idaho Session Laws chapter 99, Idaho Code 33-143. Signed March 19, 2025, effective July 1, 2025. Public schools may not display flags or banners representing political, religious, or ideological viewpoints in classrooms, hallways, entryways, or athletic fields. Exceptions include the United States, state, military, and tribal flags, school mascots and colors, brief curriculum-based use, and items worn by students. The remedy is removal, and there is no penalty.

Bill · Statute

Montana, H.B. 819 (2025) Enacted

2025 Montana Laws chapter 731, MCA 1-1-545 through 1-1-547. Effective on passage and approval in 2025. Covers all government property, including public schools and universities. Allows 11 listed categories of flags and separately bars flags representing a political viewpoint. Private speech and employees’ personal clothing are exempt. There is no enforcement mechanism or penalty.

Related recent bills

2025 House File 694 Not enacted

Would have allowed only specified flags on or in public buildings, grounds, and vehicles, with a civil penalty of $10,000 a day and possible withholding of discretionary funds. Exceptions included museums, schools, and universities. Introduced and referred to Ways and Means on February 28, 2025. Not enacted.

2026 House File 2026 Not enacted

Would have provided that custodians of public schools and state-owned buildings may raise only the United States, Iowa, and POW/MIA flags. A subcommittee meeting was cancelled on February 12, 2026. The bill was not enacted.
