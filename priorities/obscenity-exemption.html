Priority 8 of 8

Obscenity-law exemptions for schools and libraries

Last updated: September 27, 2026

Goal

Remove the exemptions in Iowa’s obscenity law that shield schools and public libraries.

Current law

Chapter 728 is a criminal statute. A separate education-law standard already applies to school libraries. Public libraries do not have a parallel regime in the education chapters.

Obscenity, school-library, and related provisions. Links open the current official PDF or the cited opinion.
Section What it does now
Iowa Code 728.1(5) Defines “obscene material” using contemporary community standards, including what is suitable material for minors, and a lack of serious literary, scientific, political, or artistic value.
728.2 and 728.2A Knowingly disseminating or exhibiting obscene material to a minor is a serious misdemeanor. A repeat offense is an aggravated misdemeanor with a one-year mandatory minimum, and then a class “D” felony with a three-year mandatory minimum. Penalties were strengthened by 2025 House File 306 (2025 Acts chapter 109). Section 728.2A covers a minor distributing obscene material to a minor.
728.7 The exemption. The section reads: “Nothing in this chapter prohibits the use of appropriate material for educational purposes in any accredited school, or any public library, or in any educational program in which the minor is participating. Nothing in this chapter prohibits the attendance of minors at an exhibition or display of art works or the use of any materials in any public library.” The first sentence is limited to “appropriate material for educational purposes.” The closing clause, on the use of any materials in any public library, is an unqualified library exemption.
728.10 An affirmative defense to a dissemination-to-minors charge if the defendant reasonably believed the minor was 18 or older based on an apparently official identification, or if the minor was accompanied by a parent or spouse aged 18 or older.
728.11 Preempts local obscenity regulation. Chapter 728 is the sole regulation of obscene material, so a city or county may not legislate on it.
256.11(9)(a)(2) and (3), 256.11(19), and 281 IAC 12.3(10) Under 2023 Senate File 496, school library materials must be age-appropriate. Age-appropriate material excludes a description or depiction of a sex act. Enforcement runs through the Department of Education and the Board of Educational Examiners. On April 6, 2026, the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit vacated the preliminary injunction against this provision in Penguin Random House v. Robbins, No. 25-1819. Opinion.
279.77, 279.81, and 280.22(2)(a) Section 279.77 requires book-removal and review procedures and a posted library list. Section 279.81 provides that students may not serve on a library-removal committee. Section 280.22(2)(a) provides that students may not distribute obscene material.
2026 House File 864, new chapter 554J (2026 Acts chapter 1143) Approved June 1, 2026. Defines material that is “pornographic for minors,” using a serious-value test applied for minors. The chapter requires age verification for websites and creates civil liability. Iowa has no separate “harmful to minors” crime in chapter 728. The chapter 728 definition is the obscenity standard with a component for minors.

The gap

  • Whether the school clause in section 728.7 protects a given material depends on “appropriate material for educational purposes.” That phrase is not defined.
  • The public-library clause is blanket. It covers “the use of any materials in any public library.”
  • The 2023 Senate File 496 standards apply to school libraries through education law, not through the criminal code. Public libraries have no parallel regime.

Points for counsel and the Legislative Services Agency before a draft is filed:

  • Repealing section 728.7 would expose librarians and teachers to criminal liability, including the mandatory minimums in section 728.2.
  • The “knowingly” element, and the rights of adult library patrons, raise First Amendment questions. Material that is obscene for minors is not necessarily obscene for adults.
  • A regulatory approach, such as access controls or parental consent, can be weighed alongside criminal exposure, or instead of it.

Proposed amendment approach

  1. Section 728.7. Repeal it, as 2026 Senate File 2119 would have done, or narrow it. A narrower bill would strike the unqualified library clause and define “appropriate material for educational purposes,” for example by cross-reference to the age-appropriate standard in section 256.11(19).
  2. A public-library regime outside chapter 728, or in the library chapters. 2026 House File 2309 used a parental-consent model. It was not enacted. That approach is a non-criminal complement to a change in section 728.7.
  3. Section 728.1(5). Optionally align the standard for minors with the “pornographic for minors” definition in new chapter 554J, from 2026 House File 864.

Related recent bills

2026 Senate File 2119 Not enacted

2026 House File 2309 Not enacted

Would have required parental consent before a minor could access certain public-library materials. A subcommittee recommended passage on February 12, 2026. The bill was not enacted.

2025 House File 306, 2026 House File 864, and 2023 Senate File 496 Enacted

House File 306 raised the penalties in section 728.2 (2025 Acts chapter 109). House File 864 created chapter 554J (2026 Acts chapter 1143). Senate File 496 set the school-library age-appropriate standard (2023 Acts chapter 91).
