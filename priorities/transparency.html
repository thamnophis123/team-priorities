Priority 4 of 8

Transparency: open records, open meetings, and posted curriculum

Last updated: September 27, 2026

  1. Goal
  2. Current law
  3. The gap
  4. Amendment approach
  5. Sample language
  6. Recent bills

Goal

Strengthen procedures of the Iowa Public Information Board and Iowa’s open-meetings and open-records laws in chapters 21, 22, and 23. Expand what parents and the public can see without a special request, including curriculum and course syllabi at regents universities and community colleges, undergraduate and graduate.

Current law

Chapters 21, 22, and 23 already give the public a right to meetings and records and give the Iowa Public Information Board enforcement tools. 2025 and 2026 laws raised some penalties and changed fees, notices, and a new requester injunction. School posting duties in section 279.77 stop short of lesson plans.

Open-government and school-posting provisions. Some 2026 changes are cited by Act because they are not yet in the Iowa Code 2026.
Section What it does now
Iowa Code 22.2 and 22.3 The right to examine and copy public records. As amended by 2026 House File 2490, a custodian may not require the requester to appear in person; must make every reasonable effort to provide records that take under 30 minutes at copying cost only; must communicate expenses on receipt; and may use legal costs only for redacting confidential information. There is no deadline to acknowledge or respond.
22.7 A long list of confidential records, including student records at subsection 22.7(1) and personnel records at subsection 22.7(11). 2026 House File 2490 made separation dates and severance amounts public.
22.8 Injunctions to restrain disclosure. Subsection 22.8(4) allows a good-faith delay while deciding whether a record is confidential, not to exceed twenty calendar days and ordinarily ten business days.
New 22.8A (2026 House File 2490) A district court may enjoin or limit a “vexatious requester” on clear and convincing evidence. News media, and a large volume of requests alone, do not qualify. The requester receives fees if the government loses. The injunction is new. Legislators may want to consider how it applies to frequent parent requesters and whether further safeguards are warranted.
22.10 Civil enforcement of the open-records law. Damages are $100 to $500, or $1,000 to $2,500 if the violation is knowing, plus attorney fees. A repeat violation can result in removal from office.
21.4, 21.6, and 21.12 Meeting notice, strengthened by 2026 House File 2490: posting that stays visible, website posting, and an “AMENDED” marking on a changed agenda. Enforcement damages were raised in 2025, by House File 706, to $500 to $2,500, or $5,000 to $12,500 if the violation is knowing. Newly elected or appointed members must complete open-meetings and open-records training.
23.6 Powers of the Iowa Public Information Board: rules, declaratory orders, investigations, subpoenas, orders with the force of law, civil penalties aligned with sections 21.6, 21.12, and 22.10, training, an annual report, and recommendations to the Legislature.
23.7 A complaint must be filed within 60 days of the violation, or of when the complainant could have discovered it.
23.8 and 23.10 The board accepts or dismisses a complaint, then proceeds to probable cause and a contested case. The board cannot remove an official. See section 23.10(3)(c).
23.12 The board has no jurisdiction over the legislative branch, the judicial branch, or the governor.
497 IAC chapters 1 and 2 Board organization and complaint procedures. The board’s declaratory-order and contested-case rules in chapters 3 and 4 were rescinded effective July 1, 2026, by 2026 Senate File 2463, in favor of the Uniform Rules on Agency Procedure. See the note to 497 IAC chapter 3.
279.77 A district must post the procedure for a book-removal request, the procedure for board review, an instructional-materials review policy, and a full library book list. “Instructional materials” means textbooks and core materials. The definition does not include lesson plans.

Federal context

On August 26, 2026, the U.S. Department of Education’s Student Privacy Policy Office, writing at the direction of Secretary Linda McMahon, sent chief state school officers and superintendents a letter. The letter warned that many districts are not complying with the Protection of Pupil Rights Amendment, including a parent’s right to inspect instructional materials used in the curriculum, and with the consent rules for surveys in eight protected areas. Noncompliance can affect federal funds. Student Privacy Policy Office letter (August 26, 2026).

The U.S. Department of Education has also announced enforcement of the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act against districts that conceal a gender-transition plan from parents. Department press release.

Syllabus laws in other states

Two verified state models cover undergraduate courses. Texas Education Code section 51.974 requires a public university to post undergraduate syllabi, instructor curricula vitae, and budgets online. Ohio Revised Code section 3345.029, from 2025 Senate Bill 1, requires a state institution to post undergraduate syllabi starting in the 2026–27 year. Neither statute requires graduate syllabi. No Iowa Code provision was found that requires a regents university or a community college to post syllabi, undergraduate or graduate.

The gap

  • Chapter 22 has no acknowledgment or response deadline for a records request. The only clock is the 20-day limit in section 22.8(4) on delay while a custodian decides whether a record is confidential.
  • Chapter 22 damages now lag chapter 21. Open-meetings damages were increased in 2025. Open-records damages were not.
  • The 60-day window for a complaint to the Iowa Public Information Board is short, especially when a records dispute develops over time.
  • Section 279.77 excludes lesson plans. It does not require posting of curriculum, supplemental materials, or syllabi. Parents must request those materials. That request overlaps the federal inspection right under the Protection of Pupil Rights Amendment.
  • There is no syllabus-posting requirement for an Iowa regents university or community college, at either the undergraduate or the graduate level. The Texas and Ohio statutes cover undergraduate courses.
  • Chapter 23, as reviewed for this site, does not state that the Iowa Public Information Board may award attorney fees itself. Whether to add that authority is a question for the Legislative Services Agency.

Proposed amendment approach

  1. Section 22.3, or a new section 22.3A. Require acknowledgment within a set number of business days, an estimated completion date, and a fee estimate. 2026 House File 2330 is the model. A firm outer deadline can be added in the same place.
  2. Section 22.10(3)(b). Raise damages to match section 21.6(3)(a).
  3. Section 23.7(1). Extend the complaint window. 2026 House File 2294 would have made it 90 days. It was not enacted.
  4. Sections 23.6 and 23.10. Consider stronger remedies for the Iowa Public Information Board. Attorney-fee authority, if it is added, should be drafted expressly. It is not stated in the sections reviewed here.
  5. Section 279.77. Add posting of curriculum, supplemental materials, and lists of lesson-level material, aligned with parents’ inspection rights under the Protection of Pupil Rights Amendment.
  6. New sections in chapter 262 (Board of Regents) and chapter 260C (community colleges). Require syllabus posting. Graduate courses can be included if the Legislature wants coverage beyond the Texas and Ohio undergraduate models. No existing section is a close fit, so a new section is the likely vehicle.

Related recent bills

2026 House File 2330 Not enacted

Would have required prompt acknowledgment of a records request, a response date, a fee estimate, and notice of a delay. Passed the House. The message was sent on February 26, 2026. Referred to Senate State Government, with a subcommittee assigned on March 3, 2026. Not enacted.

2026 House File 2294 Not enacted

Would have extended the Iowa Public Information Board complaint window from 60 days to 90 days. Passed the House on February 23, 2026. A Senate subcommittee was reassigned on March 2, 2026. Not enacted.

Bill text · History

2026 Senate File 2160 Not enacted

Would have required school boards to comply with chapter 22, with the remedies in section 22.10 available. It did not advance from subcommittee.

Bill text · History

2026 Senate File 2238 Not enacted

Would have let a “secondary custodian” defer to the agency that originated the record. A subcommittee recommended amendment and passage on February 16, 2026. The bill was not enacted.

Bill text · History

2026 House File 2490 and 2025 House File 706 Enacted

House File 2490 (2026 Acts chapter 1106) addressed meeting notices, records fees, separation pay, and the vexatious-requester injunction. House File 706 (2025 Acts chapter 139) raised chapter 21 damages and added member training.
