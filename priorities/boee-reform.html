Board of Educational Examiners: oversight and investigations

Goal

Improve oversight, investigations, transparency, and accountability at the Board of Educational Examiners. The board’s statutes are in Iowa Code sections 256.145 through 256.166, not in chapter 272. The 2023 state-government realignment (2023 Iowa Acts chapter 19, Senate File 514) moved them, effective July 1, 2023. Chapter 272 now holds a transfer note.

Current law

Board of Educational Examiners statutes and rules this priority would amend. Links open the current official PDF.
Section What it does now
Iowa Code 256.146 (introduction) The board sits within the higher education division of the Department of Education and has exclusive authority over practitioner licensing.
256.146(13) Grounds to deny or revoke a license. Some crimes require disqualification, including obscenity offenses under sections 728.2 and 728.15. Other grounds are discretionary. Paragraph (13)(e) allows revocation for knowing discrimination under section 261H.2(3) or section 279.73. Sections 279.74, 279.80, and 19.2 are not listed as grounds.
256.146(14) Complaint rules. A complaint must come from a person who has personal knowledge, must meet jurisdictional requirements on its face, and is limited to the allegations stated. A three-year limitation applies, with exceptions. The matter is to be resolved within 180 days unless there is good cause. An unfounded complaint is confidential and is outside chapter 22. Public notice occurs only once probable cause is found. Similar complaints must be re-evaluated. The administrator of an investigated licensee must also be investigated.
256.146(25), from 2024 House File 2487 The process for reporting and investigating a possible felony by a school employee and serious misconduct by a licensee. Cases receive a unique tracking identifier. Status is visible only to the reporter and the subject. Confidentiality agreements and liability waivers that conceal misconduct are prohibited. An annual report to the General Assembly is due by June 30, covering counts and trends of hearings and referrals to law enforcement.
256.147 Thirteen members: four public members (two parents and one current or former school board member), eight licensed practitioners, and the director of the Department of Education or a designee.
256.149 The director of the Department of Education appoints the board’s executive director and sets the salary.
256.150 Civil immunity for a complainant, unless the complaint is malicious, and protection against retaliation.
256.158 Hearings are contested cases under chapter 17A. The complaint and investigative files are privileged and confidential. A final decision is public. A crime found in the proceeding must be reported to law enforcement.
256.160 Mandatory reporting to the board of discipline, termination, and resignation for listed misconduct, within 30 days. The definition of grooming was broadened by 2026 Senate File 273 (2026 Acts chapter 1066), at section 256.160(5)(a).
256.155 Fees. Division IX of 2026 House File 2783 lets the board keep all licensing fees. Previously, 25 percent went to the general fund. 2026 Acts chapter 1196.
279.78(4), 256.11(9)(a)(3), and 279.73(2) Existing routes from a Department of Education finding, or from a board or court finding, to a hearing before the Board of Educational Examiners.
282 IAC chapter 11 Complaints and investigations. Rule 11.3 sets jurisdictional requirements: magnitude adequate to warrant a hearing, personal knowledge, and three years. Rule 11.4(1) lists who may file: a licensed practitioner, a human-resources director, an educational organization, a local board, a parent only of a student involved, the executive director in limited cases, the Department of Transportation, and an employee of the Department of Education. Members of the general public are not on the list. Most hearing-procedure rules (11.7 through 11.32) were rescinded on July 1, 2026, by 2026 Senate File 2463, in favor of the Uniform Rules on Agency Procedure.
282 IAC chapter 25 The Code of Professional Conduct and Ethics, Standards I through VIII: crimes and sexual misconduct, drugs and alcohol, falsification, misuse of funds, contracts, unethical practice, compliance with board orders, and incompetence. There is no explicit standard on a knowing violation of the curriculum and parental-rights statutes enacted from 2021 through 2025.

On April 28, 2026, the Auditor of State released a report stating that the Department of Education refused to provide records needed to review the board’s licensing and background-check compliance. The request followed the arrest of the former Des Moines superintendent. The Auditor could not determine whether the board complied with the law. Auditor of State report.

The gap

  • Who may complain. An ordinary citizen, and a parent whose own child is not involved, generally cannot file a complaint. Section 256.146(14)(a) requires personal knowledge. Rule 282 IAC 11.4(1) limits parents to those of a student involved.
  • What the public can see. An unfounded or dismissed complaint is confidential. Case status is visible only to the parties. The annual report covers hearings and trends. It does not cover complaints received, dismissals, or processing times.
  • Which education statutes can reach the board. A knowing violation of section 279.74, section 279.80, or chapter 19 has no path to the board. Section 279.78 and the library provision reach the board only after a Department of Education finding and a second, knowing violation.
  • Governance. The executive director is appointed by the director of the Department of Education under section 256.149. The 2026 Auditor of State report shows friction over access to records for an audit.

Proposed amendment approach

  1. Section 256.146(14). Widen who has standing to complain, for example a resident or taxpayer, or a parent of any student in the district. Set an enforceable deadline for the jurisdictional decision, and require a written reason for dismissal.
  2. Section 256.146(13). Add grounds for a knowing, repeated violation of sections 279.74, 279.80, and 19.2, and of similar statutes. The alternative is a Department of Education referral path like section 279.78(4).
  3. Section 256.146(25)(k). Expand the annual report: complaints received by category, dismissals and the reasons, median time to resolution, and outcomes.
  4. Section 256.158(3). Rebalance confidentiality. One option is an anonymized public disposition.
  5. Sections 256.147 and 256.149. Board composition, and appointment or confirmation of the executive director, are the governance vehicles. Audit access can be addressed alongside section 256.149.
  6. Rules. 282 IAC 11.3 and 11.4 govern standing and the magnitude threshold. 282 IAC 25.3 is the place for a new or clarified ethical standard.

Related recent bills

No single bill filed in the 2025 or 2026 sessions was identified that comprehensively reforms the board’s investigations or oversight. The bills below are related. Bills that were not enacted would have to be reintroduced.

2026 Senate File 2405 and House File 2116 Not enacted

Presented in part as a response to the board not enforcing section 279.74. The bills use a civil suit, not a change to board procedure. They were not enacted. Detail is on the training priority.

Senate File 2405 text · History · House File 2116 text

2026 House File 2512 Not enacted

A House Education Committee bill. It would have required the board to disqualify or revoke a license for publicly celebrating any act of politically motivated violence, including the killing of Charles J. Kirk, and it included student-protest provisions. Placed on the House calendar on February 16, 2026. Amendments were filed on March 2. A fiscal note was issued on March 16. Not enacted.

Enacted measures that already changed the board Enacted

2026 Senate File 273 broadened the grooming definition (2026 Acts chapter 1066). 2026 Senate File 2218 requires verification of legal work authorization (2026 Acts chapter 1156). 2026 House File 2783 lets the board keep licensing fees (2026 Acts chapter 1196, division IX). 2024 House File 2487 and 2023 House File 430 built the employee-misconduct investigation process.
