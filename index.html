Where eight education priorities meet existing Iowa law

TEAM is a group of Iowa citizens working on education law and school policy. This site presents TEAM’s eight priorities for the 2027 Iowa legislative session, the first session of the 92nd General Assembly.

Each page shows how a priority fits inside Iowa law that is already on the books, so it can be addressed by amending existing sections rather than writing a new chapter from scratch. The pages summarize what the current statutes and rules do, where they fall short of the priority, and which sections are the most natural place to amend. They do not contain draft amendment language.

Before using these citations

  • The Iowa Code 2026 contains laws through the 2025 session. 2026 Acts, most of which took effect July 1, 2026, will first appear in the Iowa Code 2027. Until then, cite a 2026 Act by chapter and bill number.
  • More than one 2026 Act used the temporary section number 279.89. House File 2676 and Senate File 2231 are each cited that way, and Senate File 2428 has also been identified as creating a section numbered 279.89. Senate File 2428 is also the Act that adds section 279.65B on removal of disruptive students. The Code editor will assign final numbers. Cite the Act until then.
  • The Board of Educational Examiners is no longer in chapter 272. The 2023 state-government realignment (2023 Iowa Acts chapter 19, Senate File 514) moved nearly all of that chapter to Iowa Code chapter 256, subchapter VII, part 3 (sections 256.145 through 256.166), effective July 1, 2023. Chapter 272 now holds a transfer note. Amendments should target chapter 256.
  • Bills from the 91st General Assembly (2025–2026) that were not enacted are no longer pending. They are listed as drafting models and as evidence of recent legislative interest. They would have to be reintroduced.
  • This site is not legal advice. Proposals on displays, speech, and library materials can raise First Amendment questions. The Legislative Services Agency and legal counsel should review any draft.

Iowa Code links use the pattern legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/<section>.pdf, which serves the current official edition.

The eight priorities

  1. Priority 1 Required training and age-appropriate elementary instruction Annual training on recent education laws for teachers, administrators, and board members, plus clearer rules for elementary instruction, including pronouns.
  2. Priority 2 Political and ideological symbols Flags, posters, and similar displays in public schools, drafted so student speech and curriculum stay protected.
  3. Priority 3 Reporting potential crimes and school discipline Extend required reports to law enforcement beyond weapons and drugs, and connect that duty to discipline statutes.
  4. Priority 4 Transparency Open records, open meetings, the Iowa Public Information Board, and posting of curriculum and syllabi.
  5. Priority 5 Board of Educational Examiners reform Who may file a complaint, what the public can see, timelines, and which education-law violations can reach the board.
  6. Priority 6 Public funds for political or ideological uses Training and displays paid for with public money, including flags. Campaign-finance law covers elections only.
  7. Priority 7 Screen time and personal devices Build on the 2025 device policy and the 2026 kindergarten-through-grade-5 instructional-technology standards.
  8. Priority 8 Obscenity exemptions for schools and libraries The exemption in Iowa Code section 728.7, including the blanket public-library clause, and how school-library rules differ.

7. Screen time 279.88; 2026 House File 2676’s new instructional-technology section (temporarily numbered 279.89); 256.9(69) After the December 31, 2026 working-group report, extend digital-instruction limits to grades 6 through 12. Limit non-instructional screen use. Move section 279.88 toward the full school day. Strengthen content-filtering standards.
8. Obscenity exemption 728.7; 728.1(5); 728.10; 256.11(9)(a); 281 IAC 12.3(10) Repeal or narrow section 728.7, using 2026 Senate File 2119 as a model. In particular, strike the blanket clause on the use of any materials in any public library. A separate parental-consent regime for public libraries, using 2026 House File 2309 as a model, can sit alongside that change. Alignment with the “pornographic for minors” standard in 2026 House File 864 (new chapter 554J) is a further option.
